# Fresta ontology guide

**Status:** WIP user guide

This guide explains the Fresta ontology in user-facing language and shows how
each concept appears in the running system. It is intentionally separate from
the constitutional kernel: the kernel defines authority, while this guide
explains the meaning and practical role of that authority.

## Fresta as a hippocampal layer for LLMs

Fresta is not merely a harness around an LLM. It is a hippocampal layer for
bounded language-model observation, understood functionally through
second-order cybernetics: it helps consolidate experience, index it by
objective and context, retrieve relevant structure, replay prior work, and
continue after a context window ends while observing and regulating its own
filtering process.

This is a functional architectural analogy, not a claim to reproduce a
biological hippocampus, create consciousness, or replace the model. Its
concrete meaning is implemented by persistent stores, attention resolution,
lineage, checkpoints, Phi-minus, validators, Firewall, and Gatekeeper.

## Core idea

Fresta is an active observation process. It does not try to be complete or
perfect on the first pass. It maintains enough coherence to continue observing,
comparing, correcting, and revising its own filters.

**Phi (Φ)** is the constitutional openness that prevents any current account
from becoming a final totality. **F** is the filtering operation that makes
distinction and persistence possible. The Three Orders describe every bounded
object relative to an analysis:

- **O1** — the manifestation or state being examined;
- **O2** — the relation, mediation, or transformation connecting it;
- **O3** — the condition, restriction, or boundary that makes that persistence
  possible in the current scope.

These are relational roles, not permanent labels attached to things.

## How the system operates

The Firewall and Gatekeeper apply F. Retrieval proposes candidates; it does not
grant authority. The validators test whether an O1/O2/O3 chain is structurally
grounded. Accepted and excluded possibilities are both preserved: Phi-plus
records what survived, while Phi-minus records what was filtered out and why.

The Lens is the name for the system's second-order observation of this process.
It is visible in bounded budgets, remainders, repair, source-lineage checks,
conflict handling, revalidation, and controlled promotion. It does not decide
truth independently and it never closes Phi.

## Convergence and limits

An O3 may begin as a provisional restriction. Meta-analysis compares distinct
analyses and can strengthen that restriction when independent O2 evidence shows
that it explains the persistence of the O1. The result is still contextual and
revisable.

Saturation means a loss of recoverability under the current conditions, not
that the system has discovered an absolute limit. The system may reduce scope,
request grounding, preserve a remainder, change the analytical level, or defer
promotion. A score, timeout, or repeated phrase is only a signal; it cannot
establish saturation alone.

## Reading system results

User-facing summaries may be concise, but an auditable result should preserve
the object, scope, provenance, Three-Order roles, active and historical
remainders, epistemic gaps, and the reason for any Gatekeeper decision.
